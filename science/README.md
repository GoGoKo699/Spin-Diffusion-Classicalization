# Scientific account

**At what noise scale does a large spin become operationally classical, and how much information does it still carry?**

The central result is a sharp threshold for approximating isotropic spin diffusion by an entanglement-breaking channel. At the threshold, quantum capacity tends to zero while classical capacity remains within two bits of the dimensional maximum. Anisotropic bounds and a residual-entanglement theorem explain the robustness and limits of that conclusion.

## Connected reading route

Start with the model to fix the time and norm conventions. The classicalization proof then compares a heat kernel with the known random-rotation representation of coherent measurement. Its upper bound is uniform over all encodings; its lower bound uses only the two highest-weight levels.

The communication page turns that channel theorem into operational consequences. The anisotropic page tests which symmetry is essential. The residual-entanglement page separates approximation by EB channels from exact membership in that set. The interpretation page connects the results to a spin's use as a classical or quantum carrier.
